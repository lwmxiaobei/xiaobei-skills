# Copy and storyboard

## Three coordinated text layers

1. Narration explains the idea naturally. Write complete paragraphs with varied sentence length and intentional pauses.
2. Captions compress the current spoken idea into short readable pages. They do not need to repeat every filler word.
3. On screen copy provides persistent context, figures, product names, steps, and conclusions that remain useful without audio.

## Short explainer structure

1. Hook with the strongest visual and a concrete claim.
2. Define the subject in one sentence.
3. Show two or three features, facts, or demonstrations.
4. Translate features into user value or a real workflow.
5. End with a conclusion, contrast, or call to action.

## Timing heuristics

1. Treat target duration as a copy budget before narration synthesis, then lock the real timeline from measured narration audio.
2. Keep narration at least 90 percent of narrated scene time. Reserve no more than about 10 percent for all visual breathing room.
3. Begin Chinese narration near 3.5 to 4.5 characters per spoken second, then probe generated audio and rewrite when the result misses the target.
4. Change the visual every two to four seconds unless a demonstration needs continuity.
5. Show caption pages for roughly 1.0 to 2.2 seconds.
6. Keep one scene paragraph per voice clip. Do not create one TTS clip for every caption page.
7. Keep ordinary scene tails near 0.08 to 0.20 seconds and never above 0.60 seconds without a deliberate, reviewed source audio or visual reason.
8. When an exact target fails the duration gate, change the narration copy and synthesize again. Do not distribute the missing seconds as silence.

## Source selection

1. Use human action for the hook when available.
2. Use close product shots for specifications.
3. Use screen recordings for workflows and proof.
4. Use still images for names, diagrams, prices, and comparisons.
5. Avoid repeating nearly identical footage unless the crop or message changes.
6. Do not use a clip before its relevant narration begins.

## Audio modes

1. Narration replacement keeps video elements muted and uses voiceover plus music.
2. Original speech preserves source audio and uses timestamped transcription for captions.
3. Hybrid mode keeps short demonstrations or reactions, then returns to narration. Do not overlap two intelligible voices.
